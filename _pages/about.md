I'm Mitch. I'm a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Engineering Education at Virginia Tech and simultaneous master's student in Computer Science. I earned my B.S. in Electrical Engineering from Virginia Tech and worked as a software engineer for General Motors in Detroit, Michigan before returning to graduate school. My research focuses on expertise and generative artificial intelligence (GenAI); in particular, how GenAI is transforming the way expertise is developed, displayed, recognized, and trusted. My dissertation asks that question of qualitative researchers themselves: as AI systems enter transcription, interpretation, and coding, how is qualitative research expertise changing, and how can researchers still answer for their claims? It argues that accountability for AI-infused analysis has to be designed into the analysis rather than assumed of the analysts, and develops that claim across three manuscripts: a critical review of transcription in the age of AI, tracing how automatic speech recognition deferred and obscured transcription's interpretive choices; *refutable reflexivity*, a vocabulary and visual language that lets AI-infused claims be traced to the configurations that produced them and contested by someone who did not; and a comparison of my own labels with those of prompt-engineered LLMs and decision models on the figurative language instructors use to describe GenAI. [More on the dissertation →](/dissertation/) More broadly, I build and study AI systems that fit how students, researchers, and instructors actually work.
